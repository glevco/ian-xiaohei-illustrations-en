# QA Checklist

## Required checks

- 16:9 landscape format.
- Clean white background.
- Xiaohei is present.
- Xiaohei performs the central action and is not merely decorative.
- A fresh metaphor for this article, without copying an old example's composition.
- Surreal, creative, and interesting.
- Clean and spacious, with the main subject occupying no more than roughly 60% of the image.
- One core structure per image.
- Few, short, readable English labels. All visible text is English, including text on objects.
- Orange only for the main path or arrows.
- Red only for emphasis, problems, reminders, or results.
- Blue only for supplementary notes, feedback, or system state.

## Failure signals

Regenerate or edit when any of these appear:

- A top-left title such as "Common pitfalls", "Workflow", "System architecture", or "Roadmap".
- Xiaohei resembles a mascot, meme character, or cute cartoon.
- The image resembles a presentation, course slide, or formal flowchart.
- Too many elements, arrows, or nodes.
- Text expands into lengthy explanations.
- Paper texture, shadows, gradients, beige, or noise in the background.
- Real UI screenshots or futuristic interfaces.
- Major spelling errors, unreadable labels, or any non-English lettering.
- A rigid composition without a surreal metaphor.
- Excessive similarity to a composition in `assets/examples/`.

## Iteration

- Too ordinary: give Xiaohei the central action and add a strange but coherent metaphor.
- Too complex: remove nodes; keep one action and 3–5 short labels.
- Too cute: emphasize "deadpan", "blank serious expression", "not cute", and "not a mascot".
- Too much like a presentation: remove titles, borders, tidy grids, and excess arrows; use a hand-drawn scene.
- Too similar to an old example: preserve the idea while changing the main object and Xiaohei's action.
- Incorrect or non-English text: prefer a local edit; if errors are extensive, regenerate with fewer English labels.

## Delivery standard

A strong image first feels a little strange, then makes its structure understandable within a second.

If the first impression is a tutorial page rather than an absurd product sketch on white paper, it fails.
